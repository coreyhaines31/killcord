The problem

The bill arrives after the money is gone.

Usage-billed APIs don’t send a bill until it’s too late to matter. An agent loop, a leaked key, or a forgotten batch job can run all night, and you find out from an invoice.

Every provider already offers some kind of spend cap. They’re just easy to leave unset, spread across a different console for each one. Kill Cord puts the spend in one glance and gets those caps turned on.

How it works

Every bill, one glance.

Total spend sits next to a small plug in your menu bar. Click it for each provider’s today and month to date.

In the menu bar

Spend where you already look

Today’s total, or this month’s, right in the menu bar. No more opening five billing consoles to add it up.

Every provider

Today and this month, side by side

Anthropic, Google Cloud and Gemini, OpenAI, Vercel, Cloudflare, Twilio, and Neon in one panel, each on its own billing clock. Connect more than one account per provider, like a work and a personal org.

Alerts

A nudge at the amount you choose

Notifications at daily and monthly amounts you pick, per provider and in total, once per day or month.

Balances and credits

What’s left, next to what’s spent.

Plenty of the APIs your agents call are prepaid, and when the credits run out, the job stops. Kill Cord shows the balance or credits left on each one, and warns you once when it drops to the amount you pick.

  • Prepaid balances, built in. OpenRouter, xAI, DeepSeek, Kimi, fal, Stability AI, ElevenLabs, Runway, Deepgram, Bland AI, Kie.ai, Tavily, Exa, SerpApi, Bright Data, ScrapeCreators, ScrapingBee, Scrapfly, Magister, DataForSEO, Ahrefs, Firecrawl, treg, and Monid, in dollars, credits, or API units.
  • Any service with a balance endpoint. Give Kill Cord an https address and where the number sits in its JSON. It tests the call once before it saves, and marks the number as one it can’t verify.
  • Plans with no API. Enter the plan, price, and renewal date for a service like Truelist. Kill Cord marks it “not live”, leaves it out of your totals, and reminds you before it renews.

Balances are what’s left, not spend, so they never add into your totals.

Honest numbers

It tells you how fresh every number is.

Kill Cord checks only while your Mac is awake, and it never pretends otherwise. Every provider shows when it was last checked. After sleep, or offline, a number says it isn’t live until the next check.

  • Shows provider lag. Some providers run behind. Google’s billing export can be most of a day behind, and the panel says so.
  • Says what it can’t see. Neon reports usage, not dollars. Supabase has no billing API. Totals list what they leave out.
  • Never implies overnight coverage. It doesn’t check while your Mac sleeps, and checks again about 10 seconds after wake.

Caps audit

The caps that actually stop a runaway bill.

A menu bar number can’t stop spend. Each provider’s own caps can. For every provider, Kill Cord lists the spend controls it offers, says whether each is a hard stop or only an alert, and opens the exact console page to turn it on.

No. 1

See what each provider offers

Every native control, labeled hard stop, partial stop, or alert only, so you know what will actually stop spend.

No. 2

Open This Setting

One button goes straight to the right page in the provider’s console. Kill Cord never changes a cap for you.

No. 3

Confirmed, or your checkmark

Where an API can confirm a cap (OpenAI, Google budgets, Cloudflare, Twilio, Neon), Kill Cord checks it every few hours. Elsewhere it’s your checkmark, and it asks you to look again after 30 days.

  • TwilioUsage triggers on total price (Twilio has no native hard cap)Alert only

Privacy

Your keys stay on your Mac.

Each key goes to one place: the provider it belongs to. Nothing else leaves your Mac except Kill Cord’s update check.

Keys in the Keychain

Not in files, preferences, logs, or exports. Kill Cord checks a key with its provider first, then saves it to your login Keychain.

Read only

Kill Cord only reads billing and never changes a provider setting. Anthropic, Twilio, and Neon have no read-only keys, and Kill Cord says so before you save one.

No telemetry

The app has no analytics, no account, and no tracking. Its only other network request is the update check against the GitHub releases.

Questions

Before you plug it in.

Does Kill Cord stop my spending?

No. Kill Cord only reads billing and never changes a provider setting. The hard stops are each provider’s own caps, and Kill Cord’s caps audit walks you through turning them on and opens the right console page.

Does it watch my spend while my Mac is asleep?

No. Kill Cord checks only while your Mac is awake. After sleep or time offline, numbers say “Last checked 11:42p, not live” until the next check, which happens about 10 seconds after wake. That’s why native caps matter: they keep working when your Mac doesn’t.

Which providers does it support?

Anthropic, Google Cloud and Gemini, OpenAI, Vercel, Cloudflare, Twilio, and Neon for spend, plus Supabase in the caps audit. Neon reports usage rather than dollars, and Supabase has no billing API. Apify reports this usage cycle’s spend and its usage limit. Together AI, Fireworks AI, Mistral, Hugging Face, LlamaCloud, Vapi, and Retell AI report spend in dollars, and Cartesia, Browserbase, Resend, and Postmark report usage. For prepaid balances and credits: OpenRouter, xAI, DeepSeek, Kimi, fal, Stability AI, ElevenLabs, Runway, Deepgram, Bland AI, Kie.ai, Tavily, Exa, SerpApi, Bright Data, ScrapeCreators, ScrapingBee, Scrapfly, Magister, DataForSEO, Ahrefs, Firecrawl, treg, and Monid. Services with no billing API, like Groq, Replicate, Modal, or Truelist, can be entered by hand from a preset, and anything else connects as a custom service with a balance endpoint. See what each one needs.

Can I connect more than one account?

Yes. Add as many accounts per provider as you need, like a work and a personal Anthropic organization. Each one has its own name, key, alerts, and numbers.

What key do I need?

A billing key for each provider you connect. OpenAI needs an admin key. Anthropic needs a service account, personal, or Admin API key that isn’t scoped to a workspace, in an organization rather than an Individual Org. Cloudflare needs a token with Billing Read, and Google needs a service account that can read your billing export. Never paste a key into a chat or an AI agent to set Kill Cord up. If you already have, revoke it and create a new one.

Is my key safe?

Each key lives in your login Keychain and goes only to its own provider’s billing API over HTTPS. It never appears in files, preferences, logs, error messages, or exports. If a key ever leaks, revoke it in the provider’s console.

Why does Google read low?

Google’s billing export runs hours behind, typically up to a day, and starts a few hours after you turn it on. Kill Cord shows Google’s own “data current through” time so you know how far behind it is.

Why does today say “Not reported yet”?

The provider hasn’t reported that period, which isn’t the same as $0. Vercel and Cloudflare report by day, so today often reads this way.

What does it cost?

Nothing. Kill Cord is free with no account. The source is on GitHub under the Functional Source License (FSL-1.1-MIT), and each version becomes MIT two years after release.

How do I uninstall it?

In Settings › General, click Remove All… to delete every key and all local data. Then run brew uninstall --cask --zap killcord, or drag Kill Cord to the Trash. Removing keys from Kill Cord doesn’t revoke them, so revoke any you no longer need in each provider’s console.
